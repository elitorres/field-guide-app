Family Tree

How the four featured species are related. All four are flowering plants (angiosperms) and eudicots — the shared traits on the trunk — while each branch marks a trait that sets one or more species apart.

Family Tree of the Four Wild Roots Species

Trunk = traits shared by all four  •  Branches = distinguishing traits (see Key below)

KEY

Angiosperms (trunk — all 4)
Flowering plants whose seeds are enclosed within an ovary that ripens into a fruit.
Eudicots (trunk — all 4)
Angiosperms whose seeds have two cotyledons (seed leaves) and whose pollen grains have three grooves.
Rosids (mango + hemp)
A large sub-group of eudicots; mango and hemp share this more recent common ancestor, unlike lotus and ocotillo.
Aquatic habit; thermogenic flowers (American lotus)
Roots anchor in submerged mud while leaves and flowers rise above the water; the flowers generate their own heat to spread scent and reward pollinators.
Tubular, hummingbird-pollinated flowers (ocotillo)
Bright-red tubular flowers shaped for pollination by hovering hummingbirds (and carpenter bees).
Fleshy drupe (stone fruit) (mango)
A fruit with a soft, fleshy outer layer surrounding a single large, hard-shelled seed (a "stone").
Dioecious & wind-pollinated; cannabinoid trichomes (hemp)
Male and female flowers grow on separate plants and are pollinated by wind; glandular hairs (trichomes) produce cannabinoid compounds.
